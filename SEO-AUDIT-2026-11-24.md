# Lilly Looks — Full On-Page SEO Audit

**Date:** 24 Nov 2026 · **Site:** https://lillylooks.co.uk (canonical host: non-www) · **Method:** live fetch of every sitemap URL plus legacy/host variants, compared against the local repo source (`~/workspace/lillylooks2`). **Audit only — nothing was edited, committed or pushed.**

---

## (a) Executive summary

The 24 Nov SEO fix did its main job: every product now has a unique, server-rendered page with a unique title, a self-referencing canonical and valid Product + FAQPage structured data. The foundations (robots.txt, sitemap, HTTPS, 404 handling) are sound. What remains is concentrated in **four real problems**:

1. **Internal linking is effectively missing.** Every product card on the site — homepage, category pages, product pages, guides index — is a JavaScript-click `<div data-url>`, not an `<a href>`. Site-wide there are exactly **4 crawlable links to product pages** (all inside the 3 guide articles), covering **4 of 21 products**. The other 17 products have **zero** crawlable internal links and depend entirely on the sitemap for discovery, receiving no internal link equity or anchor-text signal.
2. **All 21 product meta descriptions end mid-sentence** — the generator truncates the product description at ~160 characters ("…The roller makes", "…around the eyes, cheeks"). Meanwhile the hand-written pages overshoot: homepage meta 180 chars, category meta 188, guides index 198, guide pages 178–191 — all beyond the ~160 characters Google typically shows.
3. **The 4 category URLs in the sitemap are one identical page.** All four `category.html?c=…` URLs return the same 6,658-byte shell with the same title, the same meta and a canonical pointing at bare `category.html` — i.e. the sitemap promotes 4 URLs that each canonicalise somewhere else.
4. **Every page loads a font stylesheet it never uses.** All templates load Fraunces + Inter from Google Fonts; `style.css` only ever uses Cormorant Garamond + Jost. One of the four render-blocking stylesheets on every page is dead weight.

## (b) Findings by area

### 1. Title tags

- **Product pages (21): good.** All unique, product name first, "| Lilly Looks UK" suffix, 30–59 chars (longest: *Multicoloured Rhinestone Face Gem Stickers*, 59; shortest: *Shimmer Spray*, 30). No keyword stuffing, no duplicates.
- **Homepage: too long.** "LillyLooks | TikTok Viral Beauty Shop UK – Skincare, Makeup & Beauty Essentials" = **79 chars** — will be truncated in most SERPs. It is also brand-first, so the truncation eats the keyword tail.
- **Category pages: duplicate ×4.** All four sitemap category URLs share "Shop by Category | LillyLooks UK – TikTok Viral Beauty" (54 chars) in the raw HTML. `script.js` rewrites the title per category after JS runs, but the raw HTML Google indexes first is identical for all four.
- **Guide pages: too long.** 84 chars (*Korean Bio-Collagen Face Masks…*), 80 (*Satin Bonnets…*), 72 (*Peel-Off Lip Stain…*) — all will truncate.
- **Legacy `product.html`: generic.** Raw title is "Beauty Product | LillyLooks UK – Available on TikTok Shop" for **every** `?p=` variant (see §11).
- Minor brand inconsistency: product titles use "Lilly Looks UK" (spaced) while homepage/category/guides use "LillyLooks". Cosmetic only.

### 2. Meta descriptions

- **Product pages (21): right length, broken sentences — 21/21.** All are 151–160 chars, unique, keyword-led — but every single one is a hard character-truncation of the product description and ends mid-phrase. Examples:
  - `neck-chin-tightening-cream-with-roller`: "…part of your daily skincare routine. The roller makes" (156)
  - `multicoloured-rhinestone-face-gem-stickers`: "…Easy to apply around the eyes, cheeks" (160)
  - `romantic-bear-peel-off-lip-tattoo`: "…Apply, peel and go:" (157)
  Google may rewrite these; where it doesn't, the snippet reads as cut off.
- **Over-length hand-written metas:** homepage **180**, category **188** (same meta on all 4 category URLs — the only duplicate meta on the site), guides index **198**, guides **180 / 178 / 191**. These will be truncated by Google at ~155–160 chars, losing their endings (which include the "available on TikTok Shop" qualifier on the homepage).
- No page is missing a meta description.

### 3. Headings

- **Exactly one H1 per page on all 30 pages** ✓. Product H1 = product name ✓. Guide H1s are descriptive and keyword-bearing ✓.
- **Product pages:** H2s are the same three generic labels on all 21 pages — "How to Use", "Questions & Answers", "More Products You'll Love". No H2 carries the product name or a search phrase (e.g. "How to Use the Neck & Chin Tightening Cream"). Low risk, but a missed relevance signal repeated 21 times.
- **Guide pages: hierarchy skips a level.** Article sections are marked up as **H3** ("What is a bio-collagen face mask?", "How to use one, step by step", …) directly under the H1, and the page's only **H2** is the closing "The Product in This Guide" section. Screen-reader/outline order is H1 → H3×5 → H2.
- **Category pages (raw HTML):** H1 is the generic "Shop by Category" for all four; the real per-category H1 exists only after JS renders.
- **Homepage:** H1 "Beauty that's going viral for a reason." — brand copy, no product/category keyword. One of its 8 H2s is "Privacy Policy" (a modal heading sitting in the page outline).

### 4. Image alt text

- **Coverage is complete:** across the 30 pages, 152 `<img>` tags — **0 missing alt, 0 empty alt, 0 filename-only alt**. Every product image's alt is the product name.
- **Weakness is repetition, not absence:** on each product page the same pattern repeats — main image alt = product name, and the 4 "More Products" images alt = their product names. Nothing descriptive (no "…with built-in roller applicator" style alt). Acceptable, not optimal.
- **Worst pages:** the **homepage** — its raw HTML contains **no product images at all** (the grid is JS-rendered); its only content images are two QR codes hosted on a third-party host (`i.ibb.co`), plus the two logos. **Category pages** likewise contain no product images in raw HTML.
- **Filename typo:** the lip liner's image file is `images/Waterproof Mate Lip Liner.jpeg` — "Mate" for "Matte" — referenced in `products-data.js`, the sitemap image entry and the merchant feed. Cosmetic; the product name is spelled correctly everywhere user-visible.
- **No image has width/height attributes anywhere on the site (0/152)** — see §8 (CLS).

### 5. Internal linking — the site's biggest SEO problem

- **There are no crawlable product links on the homepage, category pages or product pages.** Everywhere, a product card is `<div class="product-card" data-url="product/<slug>.html">` with a JS click handler (`attachCardLinks()` in `script.js`). Googlebot does not follow div clicks, in the raw HTML or after rendering.
  - Homepage: the `#productGrid` container is **empty** in the served HTML; 0 product links.
  - Category pages: grid rendered by JS into an empty container; 0 product links.
  - Product pages: the "More Products You'll Love" cards are static HTML but still `data-url` divs; the only product URL in the page's HTML is its own canonical.
  - Guides index: the 3 guide cards are also `data-url` divs — the guides are not crawlably linked from their own index page either.
- **The only crawlable product links on the whole site are 4**, all inside guide articles, with good product-name anchor text:
  - `guide-korean-bio-collagen-face-mask.html` → `product/korean-bio-collagen-face-mask.html`
  - `guide-satin-bonnet-for-sleeping.html` → `product/luxury-satin-bonnet-for-sleeping.html`
  - `guide-peel-off-lip-stain.html` → `product/peel-off-liquid-lipstick.html` + `product/romantic-bear-peel-off-lip-tattoo.html`
- **Consequence:** 17 of 21 product pages have **zero internal inbound links**. They are discoverable only via `sitemap.xml` (and `llms.txt`, which search engines don't crawl as a link source). They receive no PageRank flow from the homepage and no anchor-text relevance signal. The homepage's ItemList JSON-LD does list all 21 product URLs, which aids discovery but is not a substitute for links.
- What does work: footer category links are real `<a>`s on every page; guide articles link their products properly; nav links are real anchors (to homepage sections).

### 6. Canonicals, robots.txt, sitemap.xml

- **Canonicals on all 30 sitemap pages are correct**: non-www, self-referencing, matching the sitemap exactly ✓.
- **www host does not redirect.** `https://www.lillylooks.co.uk/` and `https://www.lillylooks.co.uk/product/neck-chin-tightening-cream-with-roller.html` both return **200** with no redirect to the non-www host. The canonical tag points to non-www, so Google should consolidate — but the duplicate host is live and crawlable.
- **`/index.html` also returns 200** (no redirect to `/`); its canonical points to `/`, so risk is low.
- **Sitemap:** 30 URLs, all live (200), all non-www, product entries carry image extensions ✓. **One inconsistency:** the 4 `category.html?c=…` entries canonicalise to bare `category.html`, which is **not itself in the sitemap** — Google typically drops sitemap URLs whose canonical points elsewhere, so the category section effectively has no working sitemap entry.
- **robots.txt:** `Allow: /` for all agents + sitemap reference ✓. Nothing important is blocked.
- http → https is a clean 308 ✓. Nonexistent product URLs return a true 404 with a `noindex` 404 page ✓.

### 7. Structured data

- **Product pages (21/21):** valid JSON-LD `@graph` with **Product** (name, image, description, brand, Offer with GBP price, InStock/OutOfStock availability, NewCondition) + **FAQPage** matching the visible Q&A ✓. **No AggregateRating / review markup anywhere — as intended** (deliberately excluded; nothing review-based should be added).
- **Homepage:** Organization + WebSite + ItemList of all 21 Products (each with `url`) ✓.
- **Guide pages:** Article with publisher Organization + ImageObject ✓.
- **Category pages: no structured data in raw HTML.** An ItemList is injected by `script.js` only after JS runs (and only on `category.html`).
- **Legitimate gaps (no invented data required):**
  - **BreadcrumbList** — absent everywhere. Product pages sit under Home → Category → Product and guides under Home → Guides → Guide; the data to build these breadcrumbs already exists in `products-data.js`.
  - Product schema has no `category` property, though each product's category is known in `products-data.js`.
  - (Not recommended: SKU/GTIN — the business has no verified identifiers to publish; do not fabricate.)

### 8. Page weight / speed signals (markup evidence only — no lab CWV measured)

- **HTML size:** homepage **38.6 KB** (inflated by the inline ItemList JSON-LD carrying all 21 full product descriptions); product pages **14.5–15.9 KB** (fine); guides **11.9–12.2 KB**; category shell 6.7 KB; guides index 9.1 KB.
- **Render-blocking head resources on every page (×4 stylesheets):** two Google Fonts CSS files, Font Awesome 6.4.0 CSS from cdnjs, and `style.css` (29.4 KB). **One of the two font files is entirely unused:** `style.css` defines `--font-display: 'Cormorant Garamond'` and `--font-body: 'Jost'` and references no other family — the **Fraunces + Inter stylesheet loaded on all 33 pages is dead weight**, and the used families still load 9 weights/styles between them.
- **JS on every page:** `products-data.js` (**29.4 KB**) + `script.js` (16.4 KB) — the full 21-product catalogue data is loaded even on the static product pages and guide pages that need at most a few records.
- **Images:** product JPEGs are sensibly sized (22–106 KB). Largest: `Multicoloured Rhinestone Face Gem Stickers.jpeg` **106 KB**, `Multicoloured Crystal.jpeg` **103 KB**, `Waterproof Mate Lip Liner.jpeg` 88.7 KB. `images/logo-final.png` is **66 KB** — heavy for a logo that loads on every page, and it is not lazy-loaded. Homepage QR images are hotlinked from `i.ibb.co` (third-party host: no control over its caching, speed or uptime).
- **CLS risk:** **0 of 152 images have width/height attributes**, and product-card images are injected by JS on the homepage/category pages — layout-shift-prone by construction.
- **Preloader:** every page covers content with a `#preloader` overlay that hides only on window `load` (+250 ms) — a perceived-performance negative visible in the markup.
- Below-the-fold card images do use `loading="lazy"` + `decoding="async"` ✓; the main product image correctly is not lazy ✓.

### 9. Mobile basics

- **Viewport meta present and correct on every page** (`width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0`) ✓. `lang="en-GB"` ✓. Six media queries in `style.css` (1080 / 900 / 760 / 480 px + reduced-motion) ✓.
- **Small-text flags in `style.css`:** font sizes of **0.72rem (~11.5 px)** and **0.76rem (~12 px)** are each used by multiple rules, and 0.8–0.86rem by many more. Where these land on descriptive/label text they fall under the size mobile readers (and Google's mobile-friendly guidance) consider comfortable. Which elements they hit should be checked visually before changing.
- **Tap targets:** not measurable from markup/CSS alone with confidence — no explicit minimum-height/min-width rules were found for footer/nav text links, which are plain inline anchors. Flagged as "verify in a device check", not asserted as a defect.

### 10. Content depth

- **Product pages: 504–597 visible words each** — but only roughly **160–210 words are unique to the product** (description ~55–70, 4 how-to steps ~55–65, FAQ ~50–75). The rest is shared template: announcement bar, nav, footer, and the "More Products" block repeating 4 other products' full descriptions. Two randomly chosen product pages share **~49% of their unique vocabulary**. Thinnest pages by total words: `color-changing-jelly-lipstick` (504), `multicoloured-crystal` (511), `portable-mini-handheld-fan` (511).
- **FAQ depth is uneven:** 9 products have 3 Q&As; **12 have only 2** (incl. `nail-gem-stones-face-gems`, `mini-travel-makeup-brush-set`, `soft-makeup-powder-puff-set`, `shimmer-spray`, `mini-finger-puff-for-foundation`, `portable-mini-handheld-fan`, `waterproof-brow-gel`, `waterproof-matte-lip-liner`, `viral-18-color-eyeshadow-palette`, `romantic-bear-peel-off-lip-tattoo`, `color-changing-jelly-lipstick`, `vegan-8pcs-mini-travel-makeup-brush-set`).
- **Guide pages: healthy depth** — 796 / 755 / 832 words of original how-to content. The gap is coverage: **3 guides for 21 products**; the other 18 products have no supporting content.
- **Category pages: 148 visible words** in raw HTML (the per-category intro text exists in `products-data.js` but is JS-rendered only).
- **Homepage: 748 visible words** of brand/trust/about copy ✓ — but zero product descriptions in HTML (JS grid), so the page's product relevance is carried by JSON-LD alone until rendering.
- No verbatim duplicate body content between different products' unique sections was found — descriptions, how-tos and FAQs are product-specific. The duplication is template-level, not content-level.

### 11. Legacy `product.html?p=` pages

- **What a crawler sees without JS:** a generic shell — title "Beauty Product | LillyLooks UK – Available on TikTok Shop", a **self-referencing canonical to `https://lillylooks.co.uk/product.html`**, an **empty `#productDetail` container**, 143 words of template, and an `og:image` of the Korean Bio-Collagen mask regardless of the `?p=` value.
- **With JS** (`renderProductDetail` + `updateProductMeta` in `script.js`): for a valid `?p=`, the title, meta description, **canonical (rewritten to the static `/product/<slug>.html`)**, OG/Twitter tags and Product+FAQ JSON-LD are all set correctly. Google's rendered crawl therefore consolidates these URLs onto the static pages — the design works for Google.
- **Remaining risks:**
  1. **Non-rendering consumers** (social scrapers, some search engines, link checkers) see the generic shell and the wrong share image for every product.
  2. **Soft-404 pattern:** an invalid or missing `?p=` renders a "We couldn't find that product" page under the generic title with the self-canonical still in place — an indexable-looking error page if such a URL is ever linked or submitted.
  3. Nothing on the current site links to `product.html?p=` anymore (cards, sitemap and `llms.txt` all use the static URLs), so exposure is limited to old external/shared links — low but non-zero risk, and the fix is cheap (see P2-7).
